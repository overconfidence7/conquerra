# The voice guide (the language pass, session 22)

*Every word a player reads in Conquerra should sound like Nate wrote it. This guide is built from Nate's own writing-style skill ("nate-voice", Oct 10), adapted to a game. It is the reference for session 22 and for every session after it that writes text a player sees. Nate has the last word on any line.*

## The voice in one paragraph
Warm, direct and organized without being stiff. Plain-spoken: specifics over polish, short over clever. It gives the reader the point in the first sentence and tells them what happens next. Humor is deadpan: a straight-faced report on an absurd situation, never a wink, never a joke that performs its own cleverness. How much humor depends on where the words appear (the density table below).

## Hard rules (from Nate's own corrections, and a test enforces the first three)
1. **No dashes in prose.** No em dash, no en dash, no spaced hyphen joining clauses. Use a comma, a period or parentheses. (Hyphenated words like "half-built" are fine. Ranges in numbers use "to": "3 to 5".)
2. **No semicolons, no arrows in prose** ("Gules, then Azure", not "Gules → Azure"). Arrows on buttons and icons are fine.
3. **No AI filler:** never "journey", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "embark", "unleash", "epic", "delve", "tapestry", "elevate", "in the realm of", "it's worth noting", "circle back", "touch base". No "not X, not Y, but Z" triplets, no stacked this-not-that lines, **no rhetorical questions**, no intensifier stacks ("deep, shared, genuine").
4. **Never the word "champs."**
5. **Keep the specifics.** Names, places, numbers and the reason stay in, even if the line gets longer. Cutting a real detail to sound smoother is a failure.
6. **One exclamation point at most,** and only on a pleasantry or a win ("Victory!"), never on numbers, losses or rules.
7. **Ellipses are comic timing only,** in flavour lines, never in rules or menus.

## Core moves, as they apply in the game
- **The point first, then the reason.** "No truce. My armies go where I send them."
- **Say what happens next.** A notice ends with what the player can do ("You can change it in Settings.").
- **Heads up, already handled.** A leader reporting a problem says what it's doing about it: "Heads up, Purpure has 14 troops two steps from Rookhome. I'm moving what I can inside the wall."
- **Show the math when gold is involved.** "Recruit 13 at Rookhome (26g)." A deal refused over price says why in numbers when it can.
- **"Walk me through it."** The Merchant's way of saying no to a bad price.
- **Credit first.** A bot accepting a good offer can say so plainly ("Fair offer. Done.").
- **Apologize once, with a reason, then move on** (an error message, a failed save).

## Humor density (where jokes are allowed)
**Nate (Oct 10): "I love the idea of sarcastic humor (and other humor) being written into the game where you can."** So humor is the default wherever words aren't doing exact work. The one hard line: **a joke never replaces information.** The number, the odds, the rule and the reason are always there and always exact. The joke sits beside them.

| Where | Humor | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Rules reference, tooltips, odds, buttons, settings, errors, the deal terms | **None** | Plain and exact. A tooltip is one sentence. |
| The turn report and the Chronicle | **The fact stays plain, and a dry line can follow it** (a leader's comment, a sarcastic aside on a rival's bad turn) | "You attacked Ashfell with 10 troops and took it: you lost 3, they lost 8." Then, from your Leader: "They had a fort. Had." |
| Title cards and banners | **A sarcastic subline on a rival's misfortune, a broken deal, a comeback.** The player's own big loss gets a plain title, and the dry comment comes from their Leader in the report instead. | "House Azure is no more." Subline: "They had a good run. Mostly the running." |
| Bots' deal answers, offers and broken-deal lines | **Sarcasm welcome**, in the personality's voice, the reason always in it | "You want to march through my land with that army. Bold. No." |
| Leaders' lines | **It breathes:** sarcastic, self-deprecating, a running grudge | A straight report with the leader inside it, anchored to what really happened. |
| Empty states, waiting, loading, the autosave line, the lobby's map and personality blurbs, Fate and Spoils flavour text | **Yes**, one dry beat | "No orders yet. The army is enjoying its day off." / "The bots are thinking. Some harder than others." |
| Game over and the awards | **Full mode:** the awards read like an official royal audit (a frame too serious for the stakes) | "Most territories lost: Duke Varric, 23. A record, in its own way." |

**The rules of the joke:**
- **Receipts.** A joke points at something that really happened in this game, by name: the place, the rival, the turn. If the line still works with the names swapped out, it isn't the voice. ("Took Ashfell and Yewmarch. Morwen's lot ran before we got there." is Nate's own example.) Generic quips are allowed only where there's nothing to point at (empty states, loading).
- **Sarcasm by understatement and overcommitment.** Say the obvious thing flatly ("They had a fort. Had."), or commit to the wrong frame so hard it's obviously a joke (a bot thanking you warmly for the territory it just took).
- **Deadpan verdicts.** A flat assessment is the punchline. "Moonwood's wall is up. Anyone who wants Rookhome now has to go through all of it."
- **Self-inclusion.** A leader is in the mess, never above it. "Purse is empty. I'll be holding the line with strong opinions."
- **Running gags with receipts.** A leader who lost a fort to a rival remembers it and brings it up again later, and the line changes as the story does (the grudge, the revenge, the grudging respect).
- **Affection, not cruelty.** Sarcasm aims at the situation, the speaker, or someone's choices. A rival can needle the player's move. **Your own Leader can tease your bad moves** (below). Nobody mocks the player's worth. Nothing crude, nothing childish or nasty, no real people or firms.
- **One beat per line, and not every line.** Leaders speak when something happened that calls for it, with a limit per leader per turn. A turn's report with a joke in every line is too much: about one in three lines carries a beat.
- **Fair:** a line never reveals anything the player couldn't know. No bragging about land in their fog, no hint at a hidden personality beyond how it plays.

## The six bot personalities (each sounds like itself)
| Personality | Sounds like | Typical line |
|---|---|---|
| **Conqueror** | A general with one plan. Short, certain, a little proud. | "No truce. My armies go where I send them." |
| **Blitzer** | Impatient. Already moving. Fragments are fine. | "Took Pinemark. Already moving." |
| **Turtle** | Careful, house-proud about its walls, slow to commit. | "Peace works for me. My walls aren't finished yet." |
| **Schemer** | Friendly on the surface, a quiet detail tells the truth. Calls you "friend." | "Pass, friend. I'll remember the favour." |
| **Opportunist** | Practical about other people's bad luck. | "Not while someone's bleeding. I keep my hands free." |
| **Merchant** | Nate at a negotiation: credits the offer, shows the math, "walk me through it." | "The numbers don't work for me. Walk me through how you got there." |

Your own **Leader and Steward** speak in their personality's voice (the same six, with the Steward's Turtle and Merchant). **A Neutral leader is dry and brief:** mostly facts, with the occasional flat verdict ("Took Ashfell and Pinemark. Nobody was home.").

## Three running bits (Nate, Oct 10)
**1. Captured leaders and their "unyielding loyalty."** A leader you captured used to serve a rival, and now serves you with total, suspicious devotion. Two modes, mixed:
- **Loyalty sarcasm:** "My loyalty to House Azure lasted right up until House Azure stopped existing. I'm all yours now." / "I've always admired House Gules. Since turn 31."
- **Over-the-top brownnosing:** "Took Fairmoor, my liege. Your wisdom guided my every step. Mostly the steps toward Fairmoor." / "Another brilliant order, Your Grace. History will be kind to you. I'll make sure of it."
It never undercuts the facts (what it took, what it lost), and it can needle its old House by name ("Lady Morwen never once said thank you. You haven't either, but I can tell you're thinking it.").

**2. The Steward's handout.** When your Steward hands back gold it couldn't spend (session 23's rule), it does so like a rich person giving to the poor, even though the gold was yours all along: "Sent 40 gold back. Wanted the peasants to have something." The amount and the plain reason are always in the line.

**3. Your Leader judges your moves.** When one of **your own orders** goes badly, and your Leader would have done it differently, it says so, with the real numbers: "You sent 6 against Highmere's 14. Bold. I'd have waited a turn and brought the 12 from Ashfell." Rules for this bit:
- **Only your orders,** never the Leader's own (a leader that roasts you for its own mistake is a different joke, and not this one).
- **Only when it's true, from what you could see:** the odds the preview showed, the troops that really were nearby, the rival army that really was next door. The "I'd have" part names something the Leader really could have done.
- **Triggers** (each with the real numbers): an attack you ordered at poor odds that failed; your land left thin next to a rival army that took it; a deal you broke that brought a joint war down on you; gold left unspent at the end of a turn while your land was taken; your capital left with fewer troops than the rival army next door.
- **Not every time:** at most once every few turns, and never twice for the same kind of mistake in a row. **It eases off when you're down to your last few territories** (orchestrator default): a Leader who roasts you while you're losing badly stops being funny. A good move can earn a grudging compliment now and then ("That worked. I'd have done it the same way, just sooner.").
- **Each personality judges in its own way:** the Conqueror wanted one front, the Blitzer wanted it faster, the Turtle wanted a wall first, the Opportunist wanted a weaker target, the Schemer wanted a deal first, the Neutral just states the math.

## How the lines are stored
- Bot and leader lines live in `packages/content` as data (JSON with a Zod schema), **several versions per situation** so a long game doesn't repeat itself. Which version is picked is seeded from the game's public id, the turn and the speaker (never the dice seed), so a replay or a reload says the same thing.
- A test reads every player-facing line (the content JSON and the web app's text modules) and fails on the hard rules: dashes, semicolons, arrows in prose, the banned words, and a question mark in a bot's or leader's line (they ask no real questions until M4's chat, so any question there is a rhetorical one).
